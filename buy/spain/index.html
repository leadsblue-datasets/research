strict tier — what Spain email-marketing law requires #

Spain sits in the strict compliance tier. In practice, this requires explicit opt-in or a documented legitimate-interest basis before cold contact.

Regulator: Agencia Española de Protección de Datos (AEPD).

Standard requirements across most jurisdictions, Spain included:

  1. Sender identification. Your "From" name and reply-to must accurately identify your company. No misleading subject lines.
  2. Working unsubscribe. Every email needs a one-click or one-step opt-out. Honour requests within the legal window (most regimes: 10 business days).
  3. Honest content. Subject lines must reflect the body; no bait-and-switch.
  4. Recipient address legitimacy. Send to verified business addresses only. Under Spain's strict regime, document the legitimate-interest basis (or consent) for each recipient before send.

This page is not legal advice. Read Agencia Española de Protección de Datos (AEPD)'s published guidance before launching a sequence, or consult a privacy lawyer in ambiguous cases.

Recommended workflow for Spain cold email #

  1. Re-verify the list at the point of send. Run the CSV through NeverBounce, ZeroBounce, or MillionVerifier within 14 days of launch. Drop hard bounces. This is the single highest-impact deliverability step.
  2. Authenticate the sending domain. SPF + DKIM + DMARC all passing. Spain ESPs (and global gatekeepers like Gmail and Outlook) increasingly reject unauthenticated cold sends.
  3. Build a 3–5 touch sequence. Space touches 4–7 days apart. Spanish B2B cold email in Spanish significantly outperforms English for domestic companies; English is appropriate for multinational regional offices.
  4. Send during Tuesday, Wednesday, 9:30-11:30am local time. Timezone alignment matters more than absolute send time — schedule against the recipient's local morning.
  5. Track open, reply, bounce, unsubscribe. Iterate on subject lines first, opening lines second, sequence cadence third.

Decision-maker context. Spanish B2B decisions are relationship-driven and often require multiple face-to-face interactions before commitment. The Gerente (General Manager) or CEO makes final purchasing decisions; procurement departments handle vendor management in large companies. Titles to target: Director General, Gerente, Director de IT, Director Comercial, Responsable de Compras. Spanish-language outreach strongly outperforms English. Siesta culture means 2pm-4pm local time has low responsiveness; mornings and late afternoons are better. The August holiday month (vacaciones) should be avoided entirely.
